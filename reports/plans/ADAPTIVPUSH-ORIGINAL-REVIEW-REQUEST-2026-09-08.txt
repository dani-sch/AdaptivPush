## Product north star

AdaptivPush helps people make measurable progress through progressive overload while adapting training to their goals, schedule, equipment, performance, body, and subjective readiness.

User input and scientifically supported training methods are the initial adaptive signals. Health and wearable data may become optional enrichment later.

The app must:

- Preserve user agency.
- Explain its recommendations.
- Distinguish research evidence from product decisions and implementation heuristics.
- Avoid overreacting to one readiness score or one poor workout.
- Remain fully functional without wearables, social features, or premium features.
- Prioritize long-term training progress rather than novelty, engagement tricks, or arbitrary daily changes.

## Proposed new capabilities

Evaluate the following additions alongside the complete existing FABLE-5 scope.

### 1. Public and shareable workout programs

- Users can publish a workout program and share it through a link, code, or both.
- A shared link should open the program in the app when installed and otherwise lead to an appropriate web preview and app-download experience.
- Recipients can preview and install or clone the program into their account.
- Preserve exercises, schedule, progression model, programmed rest days, rationale, and relevant instructions.
- Separate a public program template from the author’s private workout history, health data, readiness records, and personal progression.
- Determine how published programs are versioned.
- Decide whether installed copies remain pinned to the installed version, can accept updates, or can selectively merge changes.
- Consider authorship, attribution, visibility, unpublishing, deletion, reporting, moderation, and abuse prevention.

### 2. Optional social and community features

- Explore an in-app program-discovery and workout-sharing experience.
- Assess public profiles, follows, feeds, reactions, saves, comments, and activity sharing as separate modules.
- Allow social scope to be introduced incrementally.
- Do not make core training depend on social functionality.
- Distinguish sharing a reusable program from sharing a completed workout, milestone, PR, streak, or personal achievement.
- Define privacy defaults and granular sharing controls.

### 3. Program ratings and reviews

- If public discovery is implemented, users may rate and review programs.
- Assess eligibility, one-review-per-user rules, editing, deletion, moderation, reporting, aggregate scoring, verified-use indicators, and manipulation resistance.
- Determine what qualifies someone to review a program.
- Ratings and reviews must remain optional and must not be required for program installation or training.

### 4. Health and activity display

- Display steps, distance, and logged Apple Watch or Apple Health workouts.
- Treat HealthKit as an optional adapter behind explicit consent and feature gates.
- Maintain full manual parity for users without Apple devices or permissions.
- Determine which health data is display-only and which signals may safely contribute to readiness.
- Include permission revocation, stale or missing data, privacy, source attribution, duplicate activity handling, and conflicting-data behavior.
- Do not imply HealthKit currently exists if the repository only contains placeholders.

### 5. Streaks and consistency

- Add a streak or consistency feature that supports sustainable training rather than encouraging unsafe daily exercise.
- Compare workout-day streaks, weekly-target streaks, check-in streaks, scheduled-adherence streaks, and broader consistency scores.
- Recommend a model that treats programmed rest days as successful adherence.
- Avoid punishing users for recovery, illness, deloads, schedule adjustments, or a program that calls for fewer weekly sessions.
- Keep streak calculation independent from premium adaptive engines.

### 6. Programmed rest days

- Model rest days explicitly within program schedules.
- The app must recognize a rest day instead of automatically moving to the next workout.
- Home should explain that rest is planned and offer appropriate optional actions without pressuring the user to train.
- Rest days must behave correctly in adherence, streaks, schedule changes, notifications, history, analytics, and adaptive recommendations.
- Distinguish programmed rest from an unplanned missed workout.

### 7. Workout-day swapping and schedule recovery

- Users can perform a different program day than originally scheduled.
- Users can intentionally reorder or swap upcoming workout days.
- The system records:
  - the original schedule;
  - the user’s change;
  - what was recommended;
  - what was actually completed;
  - any subsequent recovery plan.
- After a deviation, AdaptivPush recommends how to get back on track while considering:
  - muscle-group overlap;
  - minimum recovery spacing;
  - primary-movement overlap;
  - recent workload;
  - programmed rest days;
  - completed and missed work;
  - the user’s future availability;
  - readiness;
  - deload state;
  - program priorities.
- Users retain final control and can accept, modify, reject, or manually replace the proposed schedule.
- Separate simple manual reordering from premium automated schedule-recovery intelligence.

### 8. Purchasable themes

- Support a theme marketplace similar in spirit to VS Code themes.
- Separate base appearance infrastructure from catalog browsing, theme ownership, purchase processing, downloads, and application.
- Preserve accessible default themes.
- Assess platform purchase requirements, entitlement restoration, cross-device ownership, refunds, revocation, previews, version compatibility, and offline availability.
- Determine whether third-party theme creation is in scope, deferred, or explicitly out of scope.
- Cosmetic purchases must never affect training data or core behavior.
